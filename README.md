# Olist Marketplace Analytics — Delivery Risk & Platform Health

An end-to-end analytics + ML project on Olist's real Brazilian e-commerce marketplace data (~99k orders, 2016–2018). Built as a consulting-style engagement: business framing first, then KPIs, then a deployable ML model — not a notebook of disconnected charts.

## Why this dataset

Olist is a two-sided marketplace, not a retailer — it doesn't hold inventory, it connects ~3,000 independent sellers to buyers and takes a commission. That structure means the interesting business questions aren't "are sales up," they're about **platform health**: is revenue concentrated in a few sellers, does late delivery damage the reputation engine (reviews) that the whole platform depends on, and can we predict and prevent that before it happens. Full business context: [`docs/business_overview.md`](docs/business_overview.md).

## Key findings

1. **Review scores collapse on late delivery**: 4.29 avg → 2.57 avg the moment an order arrives after its estimated date. In a marketplace, reviews drive future conversion for every seller — this isn't a logistics nuisance, it's a revenue-protection problem.
2. **Seller concentration risk**: the top 10% of sellers generate **67.6%** of total platform GMV. Losing a handful of top sellers to a competitor is a material revenue risk.
3. **8.1% of delivered orders arrive late** — around 7,700 of ~96,500 delivered orders in this dataset.
4. A delivery-delay risk model, scored at order time, catches **60% of truly late orders** before they ship (vs. ~5% using a naive default-threshold model — see [`docs/decision_log.md`](docs/decision_log.md) for why the naive version was actually useless despite 92% "accuracy").

## The model: delivery-delay risk

**Business objective:** score an order's probability of arriving late, using only information known at the moment of purchase — no delivery-event data, to avoid leakage — so Ops can intervene (expedite shipping, proactively message the customer) before the order becomes a bad review.

**Approach:** Random Forest, class-balanced, with a business-tuned decision threshold (optimized for recall on the "late" class rather than raw accuracy — see decision log for why this matters). Includes a leakage-safe seller historical late-rate feature computed only from the training split.

**Result:** ROC-AUC 0.735. Catches 60% of late orders at a 26% false-alarm rate. The single strongest signal is a seller's own delivery track record; the residual unexplained variance is dominated by seasonal demand spikes (e.g. November 2017 volume nearly doubles), suggesting the next improvement is carrier-capacity data, which isn't present in this dataset.

## Recommendation (business framing)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Problem** | Late deliveries (8.1% of orders) crater review scores, which drive marketplace-wide conversion |
| **Recommendation** | Score every order at purchase time; flag high-risk orders for expedited handling or proactive customer messaging |
| **Expected impact** | Partial mitigation on ~60% of otherwise-late orders per period |
| **Risk** | 26% false-alarm rate — only worth acting on if the intervention is cheap (e.g. an email) vs. expensive (free upgraded shipping for everyone flagged) |
| **Owner** | Logistics/Ops, with Data Science maintaining the model |
